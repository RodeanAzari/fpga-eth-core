## Interpretation

This exercises the whole generic TX path on hardware: `udp_rx` learns the sender's MAC/IP/port
from each received frame (no ARP), `udp_tx` builds a new Ethernet/IPv4/UDP frame around an
arbitrary payload of every length from 1 to 1,472 bytes (lengths under 18 bytes include
Ethernet padding), and `eth_tx`/`mii_tx` send it at 96% of line rate in each direction at once,
with no underrun. Every echo was checked byte for byte by the host.

The checker was fixed before this run (`79fba80`). Earlier runs on the same board reported
`lost` = `mismatched` in bursts (0 to 16% of a run) while the board's own counters showed every
datagram received and echoed. Intact echoes were arriving more than 256 datagrams late, and the
old checker had already written them off after 256 later datagrams; when they arrived, they
matched nothing outstanding. The checker now writes a datagram off only 1 s after sending it,
and drains the socket before every send. A loopback case that holds 600 echoes back and releases
them late fails on the old checker (lost = 1,042) and passes on the new one. With the fix,
runs at 8,000, 12,000, 14,000 and 15,000/s were clean before this gate run. What delays the
echoes on this host (the WSL2/Hyper-V receive path or a descheduled checker thread) is not
established.
