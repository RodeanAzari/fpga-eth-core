# eth-core-v0.3 gate: cut-through RX, 610 s at 8,000 datagrams/s

**Result: PASS, with host-side drops accounted for (accepted by the maintainer, 2026-11-13).**
In run 2 (the gate run), 4,880,000 datagrams were sent. The host's USB adapter dropped 16
before the wire, and the board received the other 4,879,984. Every RX error counter on the
board was 0, and the RX FIFO high-water mark was 1 byte (of 2,048). The board lost no frame
that reached it.

The gate in `~/fpga-coord/dependencies.toml` asks for "every datagram received". Taken
literally, run 2 misses that by 16 datagrams. Each of them is counted as a TX drop by the
host interface (`ip -s link`, before and after the run), not on the board. Received plus
host-dropped equals sent exactly. The maintainer chose to accept this accounting rather than
move the cable to the I225-LM NIC (v0.1's host path) or lower the rate.

## Interpretation

Cut-through `eth_rx` passes every frame it receives intact at 98.4% of line rate for 10
minutes. That is the highest rate the link carries with 1,472-byte payloads (ceiling
8,127/s). No CRC, PHY, length or FIFO error is counted, and the RX FIFO never holds more
than a few bytes. This matches store-and-forward v0.1 on the same test.

The only losses are in the host. In both runs the adapter's own TX-dropped counter equals
the number of datagrams missing, so they never left the host. They split as follows:

- **Run 2 (gate run):** the board's `seq_lost` = `seq_err` = 16. That is 16 single-datagram
  gaps, all at uptime 294 s, which accounts for every missing datagram. The harness waited
  for the board's MAC to be up (uptime ≥ 3 s) before sending.
- **Run 1:** 13,787 were dropped before the link came up. That run's harness started the
  sender right after programming without waiting, and the host drops while there's no
  carrier. The board's first report (uptime 0, after the MAC leaves reset at link up)
  already shows 7,638 datagrams, and its count stops rising at uptime 608 rather than 610.
  Both put the link-up about 1.7 s, or about 13.8 k datagrams, into the stream. These
  datagrams are before the first one the board received, so they aren't in `seq_lost`.
  The other 3,748 were single-datagram gaps (`seq_lost` = `seq_err` = 3,748) during the
  stream, from 18 s to 606 s, while other jobs held the host at a load of 10 to 14. The
  `cdc_ncm` driver running over usbip can't always keep up at line rate. After each burst
  the board's per-second count rises to the 8,128/s ceiling as the adapter drains its
  queue.

These runs don't show that the board would count a frame lost on the wire. The `ovf_lost`,
CRC and `rx_err` paths are covered in simulation (`make ci`, both modes). Consumers that need
every datagram at line rate from this host should use a NIC that doesn't drop at the driver
(v0.1 ran on the I225-LM: zero drops, some reordering).
